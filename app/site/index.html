01 · Find

Argon finds real failures in hours of fleet video.

Public robot data almost never labels failures. We scan raw rides from the open FrodoBots-Mini-4K dataset with physics triggers (tilt, free-fall, impact), then Argon confirms only what it can see happen, rejecting the video glitches, speed bumps and ramps that fool sensors.

02 · Understand

It explains what went wrong, and what it can't know.

Argon reads the video and the robot's IMU together, timestamps the failure, estimates the scene for a physics simulator, and lists its own unknowns instead of guessing.

03 · Rebuild

A digital twin that fails the same way.

Argon turns its diagnosis into a MuJoCo scene and proves it: the twin is accepted only if the robot's original behavior reproduces the failure. If it doesn't, Argon compares sim and reality frame by frame and revises its estimate. Then it plans dozens of realistic variants.

04 · Look real

Twins you can't tell from the real thing.

Argon rebuilds the scene in 3D from a handful of real frames, and the robot drives its exact recorded path at the real camera height and field of view. Gemini Omni then restyles the render so it looks like the real camera. Geometry and motion come from the twin; Omni only provides the look.

05 · Learn, safely

RL learns. Argon catches it cheating, then approves the real fix.

Argon writes the reward from good and bad rides, and reinforcement learning retrains the policy on the twins. Then Argon watches the result. Optimizers find shortcuts, and Argon refuses to ship them.

07 · Share

Federated robot learning

Each robot learns locally from its own failures and uploads about 100 bytes of policy update instead of gigabytes of video. That is federated learning, and on robots it has always had one blocker: no one can see the data behind an update, so no one can trust it. Argon removes that blocker. It reviews every update against every city, explains its decision in plain language, and blocks the ones that would trade safety for speed or fix one city by breaking another.

For robotics teams

Your fleet already records its mistakes.

Hindsight turns them into reproducible test cases, verified fixes and safe fleet updates, without a human scrubbing footage. Built for autonomy engineers, fleet operations and safety teams running sidewalk, warehouse and home robots.

"We log terabytes and can't find the failures."
Automatic failure mining with timestamps, cause and visual evidence. Glitch-resistant: 15/15 correct on human review.
"We can't reproduce field failures in sim."
A twin that reproduces the failure, checked against reality, plus dozens of variants. Every field failure becomes a regression test.
"The retrained policy passes the tests but cheats."
An AI reviewer watches before/after behavior and blocks gamed fixes. It blocked 7+ policies before approving the real fix.
"An update fixed one site and broke another."
Per-site gating: every update is checked against every city's scenarios before rollout, with a written reason.
"Uploading all footage is costly and a privacy risk."
Upload only what matters: failure moments (53× less) or just policy updates (~770,000× less). Bystander footage stays on the robot.
"The long tail is too rare to train on."

The agents

A team of agents, led by Argon.

Each agent does one job. Argon orchestrates: it decides, writes code, builds worlds and has the final say on safety. The others are tools it delegates to.

gemini-4-argon

Argon: lead agent

Perceives, reasons, codes and judges across the whole loop.

  • Failure auditor: confirms only failures it can see, rejects glitches
  • Diagnostician: fuses video + IMU into a timestamped cause
  • Twin builder: turns the diagnosis into physics and revises until it reproduces
  • Scenario planner: chooses which variants to generate
  • Reward engineer: writes the RL reward from good and bad rides
  • Safety verifier: watches the retrained robot, catches reward hacking
  • Fleet gatekeeper: accepts or rejects each robot's update
  • World builder: writes the 3D twin scene from a few real frames

Why Argon

What would not be possible without Argon.

Every step below needs one model to watch video, read sensor logs, write and debug code, and reason about physics in a single loop. Without Argon, each of these is a team of specialists, or it doesn't happen at all.

  • Video + sensor fusion with honest uncertainty. It confirms failures only when it can see them, and calls out telemetry glitches.
  • Self-correcting twins. It compares sim to reality and revises the physics until the failure reproduces.
  • Rewards written from observation, then defended against an optimizer that is actively trying to game them.
  • A skeptical reviewer that blocks unsafe policies and unsafe fleet updates, inside an autonomous sandbox loop.
  • Full 3D scenes from a few frames, matched to a real camera.
